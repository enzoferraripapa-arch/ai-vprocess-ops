# Application Examples And Responsibility Boundary

The examples in this repository are application examples only.

They are not product instructions, regulatory instructions, safety approvals,
legal advice, quality-system approval, cybersecurity approval, or a guarantee
that a resulting product is safe, compliant, marketable, or fit for a specific
use.

## What The Examples Are For

Use the examples to understand how an AI-assisted engineering-memory workflow
can be shaped:

- preserve the reason behind generated work;
- separate candidates from controlled records;
- route uncertain output to human review;
- prepare evidence packages before formal ALM or SOP entry;
- show an AI coding agent what it should build for a project-specific pipeline.

They are intentionally small and fictional. They demonstrate a pattern, not a
complete product-development process.

## Example Uses

| Example use | What this repository can help with | What remains your responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI coding on a long-running app | Preserve requirements, decisions, trace candidates, tests, and open issues across sessions. | Decide architecture, security, release readiness, and production operation. |
| Vibe-coded prototype cleanup | Convert intent, generated code, and observed behavior into reviewable requirement and trace candidates. | Decide what is real product scope and what must be rewritten, tested, or discarded. |
| Regulated or safety-related development preparation | Build a pre-formal review package before records enter Polarion, DOORS, Jama, Codebeamer, or another controlled system. | Apply the actual standard, customer rule, QMS process, approval workflow, and competent review. |
| Legacy-system recovery | Import authorized observations, tests, logs, and documents into a candidate graph. | Confirm ownership, authorization, confidentiality, and whether inferred behavior should become a requirement. |
| Engineering review support | Produce bounded prompts, impact paths, review reports, and one-way handoff packages. | Accept, reject, sign, baseline, release, or audit the work through your own authority chain. |
| Local-first agent memory experiment | Copy the schema and prototype as a starter pattern. | Operate, secure, back up, and validate the resulting project environment. |

## Responsibility Boundary

If you use this repository to develop, review, release, sell, certify, operate,
or maintain anything, the decisions and consequences are yours.

You must decide, verify, and document at least:

- whether the workflow is appropriate for your product and risk class;
- whether the generated or imported artifacts are correct;
- whether the reviewers are competent and independent enough for the context;
- whether laws, standards, customer requirements, accessibility rules,
  cybersecurity obligations, privacy rules, store policies, and contractual
  obligations apply;
- whether the formal ALM, QMS, release, and audit records are complete;
- whether tests, inspections, analyses, simulations, and field evidence are
  sufficient for your use;
- whether deployment, rollback, monitoring, complaint handling, and support are
  ready.

This repository does not operate your process, approve your evidence, certify
your product, or absorb your project risk.

## Do Not Treat Examples As

- proof of compliance;
- production readiness;
- a safety case;
- a cybersecurity case;
- a privacy assessment;
- a legal assessment;
- a quality-system record;
- a formal ALM adapter;
- a substitute for competent engineering review;
- permission to use unauthorized third-party material.

## Practical Rule

Use the repository like a map, not like a permit.

It can help you see which candidates, traces, gates, decisions, and handoff
packages should exist. It cannot make those items true, approved, complete, or
safe.
